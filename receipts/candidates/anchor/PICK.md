# anchor: picked B (3,794 tris, verify ok, 2.56 x 1.74 x 1.71 m)

B won by eye: the arms, crown and spade flukes are one bevelled crescent profile, so the silhouette from the front is the storybook anchor of the reference (deep U arms, broad barbed spades, shank rising about 35 degrees to a flared head with a real eye the ring passes through). The lathe stock with knob ends reads from every side, and the chain drops from the ring into a conical heap beside it. A (primitives, 2,832) has the same pose, but its torus arms and 4-sided cone flukes read thin, more like a hook than an anchor. C (tapered tube sweeps, 3,312) uses a different reading: it rests on crown, one fluke and one stock end, so it lies lower and more naturally. From the front, though, it reads as a jumble of crossed bars, and it is 3.1 m deep.
Pose: the roll is solved numerically so the crown underside and the +X fluke tip share the ground plane, then yawed -8 degrees. Ground for the chain heap is measured without the kelp tufts.
Loader check: load_proof_b.png loads it through game/assetlib.js ASSET() (merged, no keepHierarchy). Tris are 3,794 before and after, so all 59 chain links survive the instance expansion, in 5 draw calls with min y 0. Scratch page: _scratch_d/anchor/load.html + load.mjs.
Expect: height 1.75, width 2.6 (the shank is 2 m long; tilted, it stands 1.74 m).
Known weakness: the flat extruded profile reads as a slab (about 15 cm thick) from pure side views, where a real anchor arm is round. The chain hangs dead plumb from the ring to the heap, with no sag. It is balanced on two contacts, which the reference also shows; a real one would be propped by its stock. Kelp is small 3-sided cones, so it reads as spikes up close.
