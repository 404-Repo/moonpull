# buoy: picked C (1,464 tris, verify ok, 0.91 x 0.81 x 0.81 m incl. rope coil; body 0.6 m across)

C won by eye: ten hand-built stave gores, each pushed out mid-stave so the seams sink into grooves, alternate roof-clay red and whitewash as separate meshes. The lobes catch the light at every seam, which is the closest match to the painted pumpkin-ribbed reference, and the bands read from all four sides. The iron ring hangs through a half-eye on a timber bung and faces the front, the rope comes off the ring over the +X shoulder and lies in one coil on the ground at the front right, kelp fronds hang on the shoulder, and a barnacle crust sits low on the front-left. It is the only one of the three under the 1,500 budget with all its parts.
A (primitives, 1,308): SphereGeometry gores. Cheapest, but a smooth ball with flat stripes, and the rope ends in a bare hook that reads as a wire.
B (lathe profile, 1,664, over budget): the squat barrel profile with a lashed collar is a good second reading, but it is over budget, the gores are smooth so it reads as a striped egg, and the eye on top looks like a spike from the side.
Iteration: the first pass of all three was 1,800 to 2,500 tris and was trimmed by segment counts, not decimation. In C, a kelp frond hung below the body and became the "ground", which lifted the rope coil 8 cm off the floor; the fronds moved up onto the shoulder. The 2 cm bung bolts were dropped (under the 4 cm floor), and the rope start was moved so it no longer overshoots above the ring.

Known weakness: there is no paint wear or chipping (flat colours only), the barnacles are small and on one side only, and the ground coil widens the footprint to about 0.9 m, so a collider should use the body (r about 0.31 m) and not the bounding box.
