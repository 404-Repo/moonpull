# fish_weir: picked A (4,651 tris, verify ok, 9.62 x 1.40 x 1.30 m)

A won by eye: its withies are 14 courses of round tubes that pass in front of one stake and behind the next (a cosine offset along the curve normal, phase flipped every course), so the wattle reads as woven rather than as rails from the front, the back and at three-quarter, and the thin uprights between stakes give the panel its basketwork rhythm. The stakes are chunky octagonal (chamfered) posts with slanted or ridged split tops, bleached driftwood or weathered timber above a dark wet sleeve, leaning slightly, which sits with the harbour kit. Two wattle runs (stakes 1 to 5 and 8 to 11, 7 of 13 bays) leave three bare stakes in the middle and two at each end, as briefed. Kelp ribbons stand at most stake feet and hang off the wattle above the ground.

B (extruded split-top stakes, hand-built lens-section woven bands, 4,134) was the densest panel and closest to the reference's tight wattle, and its notched split tops were the best stake heads, but its bands read as flat strips edge-on and the extruded stakes look boxy next to the round withies; weed was sparse. C (lathe split logs, torus-arc withies bowing alternately front/back, 4,992) read as an open rail fence at 11 courses, sat at the budget line, and its bowed arcs only read as weave from above.

Close-ups: closeup_abc.png (rows A, B, C before the final densify), closeup_a.png (final A). Gentle bow of 0.8 m toward +Z, runs along local X, no sand base, no mounts exemption.

Known weakness: foot kelp reads as upright grass tufts rather than slumped weed; no weed clumps as big as the reference's draped bunches; withy ends are open tubes (only visible right at the run ends).
